A biodata is shared widely, often by forwarding on messaging apps. That makes it worth being deliberate about what goes on the page.

## Keep these out

**Identity and financial numbers.** Aadhaar, PAN, passport, bank account and property document numbers have no place on a biodata. They can be misused if the file is forwarded.

**A full street address.** City and state are enough at the start. Share the address once the families have spoken and there is trust.

**Claims you cannot support.** Inflated income, a title you do not hold or a degree you have not finished create problems later. A modest, accurate biodata builds more trust than a polished, inflated one.

**Comments about past relationships.** If you are divorced or widowed, state marital status plainly. Detailed stories belong in a conversation, not a document.

**Long lists of demands.** A page of requirements can read as rigid. Two or three lines about values and outlook are more inviting.

**Personal social media links.** Unless you want strangers to browse them, leave them out.

## Handle these with care

- **Health details.** Blood group is fine. Deeper health information is better shared in person, at the right time.
- **Phone numbers.** Use a number that will be answered, ideally a parent's or a trusted relative's.
- **Photos.** Use a clear portrait rather than group photos or images with other people.

## A calm test

Before sharing, ask: if this file reached a hundred strangers, would I be comfortable? If a detail makes you hesitate, remove it. You can always add it back for a specific family.
