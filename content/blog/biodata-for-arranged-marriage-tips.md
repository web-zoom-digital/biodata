In an arranged marriage, the biodata often arrives before the people do. These eight habits make it work in your favour.

## 1. Be accurate

Every detail should match what you will say in person. Small inconsistencies cost more trust than modest facts.

## 2. Keep the tone warm and simple

Write the way you speak. Short, plain sentences in About me feel more genuine than formal language.

## 3. Lead with a good photo

A clear, recent portrait does more than any paragraph. See our [photo guide](/blog/how-to-choose-a-biodata-photo).

## 4. Make family details easy to scan

Parents' names and occupations first, then siblings with a short note each. Avoid long family histories.

## 5. Be clear about values

If lifestyle matters to your family, state it honestly, such as food preference or whether you smoke or drink. It saves both sides time.

## 6. Give a reachable contact

Use a number that will be answered. Decide in advance who will pick up and what they will say.

## 7. Choose a layout that suits your family

Traditional families may like a framed, gold-toned design. Others prefer a quiet, modern one. The [template gallery](/templates) has both.

## 8. Check it twice, on a phone

Most people will open your biodata on a phone. Look at the preview at small size and read it slowly for spelling and numbers before you share it.
