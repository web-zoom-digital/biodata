A biodata travels. It is forwarded between relatives and friends, and sometimes saved by people you have never met. A few habits keep you in control.

## Decide what is safe to include

Keep ID numbers, bank details and property documents off the page. Use city and state instead of a street address. See [what not to include](/blog/what-not-to-include-in-marriage-biodata) for a longer list.

## Choose who receives it

Send your biodata to people you know or to families introduced by someone you trust. Be careful with public groups and unverified accounts.

## Pick the right format

- **PDF** is best for sending to families and printing
- **PNG or JPEG** is convenient for messaging apps, and cannot be edited easily
- Avoid sending editable documents unless someone needs to change them

## Use a dedicated contact number

Where possible, list a number that you or a parent will answer, and be ready to ask who is calling and how they found the biodata.

## Keep track

Note who you sent it to and when. If you update your biodata, tell recipients to use the new version.

## Protect your draft

BioCraft keeps your draft only in your own browser. On a shared or public computer, use Clear draft in the editor before you leave, and download only what you need.

## Trust your instincts

If someone asks for documents, money or unusual personal details early on, pause and speak to a family member. A genuine family will understand you taking time.
