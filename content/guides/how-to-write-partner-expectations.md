Partner expectations are best treated as a friendly signal about what matters to you, not as a checklist.

## Focus on values and outlook

Families respond well to expectations about character and outlook: kindness, respect for family, openness to new ideas, a shared sense of humour. These are easy to agree with and easy to discuss.

## Keep it short

Two or three sentences are enough. If you need a full page to explain what you want, the conversation is better held in person.

## Examples

**Values-first**

"I am looking for a kind, well-educated partner who values family and communication, and who is open to a balance of tradition and new ideas."

**Practical and open**

"A supportive and independent partner from a similar cultural background, who enjoys a simple home life and is comfortable with both of us continuing our careers."

**Very brief**

"Someone respectful, honest and family-oriented."

## What to avoid

- Detailed requirements about looks, height or income
- Conditions that depend on someone else's family
- Comparisons with other people or past relationships
- Anything you would not be comfortable saying to the person directly

## A quick self-check

Read your expectations aloud. If they sound like a job advertisement, simplify them. If they sound like a description of a person you would enjoy spending time with, they are ready.

You can write them in the [editor](/create), or switch the section off if you prefer to discuss expectations in person.
